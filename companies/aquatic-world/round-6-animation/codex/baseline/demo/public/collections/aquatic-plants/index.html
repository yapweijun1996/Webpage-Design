The landscape, in layers.

A broad leaf catches the light. Fine stems soften an edge. Plant forms bring texture, depth and a sense of atmosphere to the scene.

Prepare a plant enquiry↗

A considered palette

Quiet greens.
A little contrast.

Combine fine and broad textures to create depth. An accent of bronze or red can draw attention to a chosen part of the composition.

Use these images to describe a mood, rather than to specify a species. Confirm plant identification and suitability with a specialist.

Plan the layers

Think from
front to back.

Sketch where texture, height and open water might sit. This is a visual planning exercise, not a planting or care prescription.

  1. Foreground · establish space

    Decide where the eye enters the scene and how much open area supports the composition.

  2. Middle · connect the elements

    Explore leaf shapes and textures around the hardscape. Look for transitions rather than isolated pieces.

  3. Background · shape the view

    Consider height and density as a backdrop. Include questions about lighting, maintenance and suitability in your brief.
