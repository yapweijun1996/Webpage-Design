## Proposed direction

Use the submerged spatial perspective of the first Green Aqua reference with the photographic detail and scene hierarchy of the second. Fish should move naturally at a scale that leaves the environment visible. Preserve foreground, middle-distance and background detail instead of making one oversized fish the entire composition. Prefer natural greens, shaded wood and clear water; retain enough quiet tonal space for the live title and CTA.

The camera may be steady or move slowly if the selected footage supports it. A fixed camera can still feel alive when animals, plants and water move naturally. Camera movement is not an automatic quality upgrade.

For the next standalone sample:

1. Review a continuous forward-moving shot at normal speed before editing it for repetition. Do not reverse animal motion or stretch a tiny segment by holding repeated frames.
2. Check believable propulsion, body/fin continuity, plant movement and the relative depth of fish and vegetation throughout the shot.
3. Aim for a usable 12–20-second take as a proposed target, subject to the actual selected footage. Duration alone does not establish quality.
4. Review the complete take and repeated joins. Do not accept a matching first/last still as proof of a pleasant loop.
5. Review a full desktop composition and a portrait composition separately before assigning copy regions. None of these references has passed a phone crop or live-UI readability check.
6. Obtain user acceptance of the standalone motion direction before distributing final identical assets and integration requirements to both CLIs.
