# Endpoint Frame Brief — Proposed, Not Generated

Create one new UI-free endpoint still per orientation, using the respective existing clean habitat as the visual reference. Keep the original files unchanged. Each new orientation would use the **same reviewed endpoint image** for its first and last frame; the actual route's acceptance of identical inputs must be confirmed before execution. This controls habitat appearance, not the fish's unseen path or the water's velocity.

## Shared scene requirements

- Preserve the planted freshwater setting, coherent root geometry, layered depth and restrained green/daylight palette. No ocean reef, tank hardware, glass reflections, overlarge fish, interface, typography or audio cues.
- Remove the three fully exposed reference fish from the endpoint composition. The planned lead fish is fully behind one substantial root-and-plant structure at both endpoints; no eye, tail, pelvic fin or silhouette should be left exposed. A provider still needs to invent its first emergence, so species and anatomy are explicitly specified in the motion prompt and checked in the result.
- Leave a plausible opening and swim corridor in front of that structure. Do not seal the gap with a solid wall of leaves. The lead fish must be able to emerge, swim at middle distance, turn and return behind the same structure without crossing live copy.
- Leaves and highlights should depict a calm moment in an ongoing current. Do not turn the whole scene into a featureless dark plate just to simplify the join.
- Keep camera, lens, wood, plants and lighting identical across the two endpoint roles. Use the exact same file bytes after review rather than independently generating a second matching picture.

## Landscape: independent 16:9 composition

Reference: [existing landscape](../../media/landscape-generation-frame.png).

- Maintain the current quiet left water and detailed right habitat. Leftmost 42% is the reading zone; top 14% is the navigation zone. These are creative composition guides, not a substitute for actual viewport testing.
- Strengthen a natural concealment pocket in the right middle-distance root/leaf structure. It must remain visible inside typical hero crops.
- Visible swim corridor: approximately x=55–88%, y=25–64% of the frame. One modest angelfish emerges, traverses a small arc and turns into depth. Its entire extended fin span must stay within the reviewed safe region.
- Prepare native 16:9 art, then proportionally normalize to 1280×720. Avoid stretching or trying to create this frame by cutting an unrelated portrait image.

## Portrait: independent 9:16 composition

Reference: [existing portrait](../../media/portrait-generation-frame.png).

- Preserve the depth corridor and upper-middle arch. Top 12% is navigation; lower 44% is the quiet reading zone. The fish should move primarily within x=22–80%, y=22–50%, with its full fin span visible.
- Place the concealment pocket at the upper-middle root/leaf arch, not at the outer right edge. The fish must disappear through believable habitat occlusion, not leave the phone crop.
- Prepare native 9:16 art, then proportionally normalize to 720×1280. This is a separately arranged composition, not a desktop crop.

## Frame review and handoff

Inspect each actual image at full size before pinning it. Overlay live-copy regions and check root continuity, room for the fish's full fin span, the opening, mobile crops and tonal readability. Save the selected image, provenance, dimensions, SHA-256 and generation/edit instructions. Register a version-pinned asset only after the image exists; this study deliberately assigns no invented asset ID or acceptance status.

Use the mandatory image-generation/editing tool for semantic changes to the photographs. Proportional resolution normalization may be a separate deterministic preparation step. Do not erase fish or repaint habitat with ad-hoc scripts. Static frame review establishes composition only; it cannot establish swimming, occlusion or loop acceptance.
